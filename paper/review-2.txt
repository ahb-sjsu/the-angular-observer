3. The "previously justified heuristically" claim about NJW is too strong. Your geodesic-preservation angle is genuinely different from the cluster-separation angle, and that's your contribution — but a referee who knows this literature will flag the framing immediately. 4. The Euclidean baseline beats you on two substrates, and the paper doesn't address it. The Wolfram framing is a venue risk. You've bounded it carefully — the discussion is honest and the curvature null is exactly the right kind of result to report. But the title's subtitle, the abstract's last sentence, and the observer-theory thread will make ML/spectral venues (JMLR, NeurIPS) nervous while the spectral content will be underappreciated at Complex Systems. My suggestion: strip observer theory from the title and abstract entirely, keep §7's second paragraph as the sole home of the interpretation, and let the substrate-universality result (which includes Wolfram-model graphs as one substrate) carry the connection. The paper is stronger as "a basis-level fact about spectral embeddings, tested on five substrates including hypergraph rewriting" than as a physics-adjacent paper.
