\begin{abstract}
The commute-time (resistance) embedding built from the low eigenvectors of a
graph Laplacian is a workhorse of spectral geometry, yet its distances are known
to \emph{degenerate} on large graphs \cite{vonluxburg2014}. We show the geometry
does not vanish---it migrates entirely into the \emph{angular} coordinate.
Writing each node's low-mode embedding in polar form (magnitude $\times$
direction), the direction preserves graph geodesics at Spearman
$\rho \approx 0.93$ on a reference manifold, while the magnitude preserves
$\rho \approx 0.03$ and a random-mode basis of equal size preserves
$\rho \approx 0$. Keeping only the angle is exactly the row-normalization of
Ng--Jordan--Weiss spectral clustering \cite{ng2002}\rev{, whose existing theory
concerns cluster separation \cite{schiebinger2015}; we give it a geodesic
justification. We show, as a corollary of von~Luxburg--Radl--Hein, that the
radial coordinate of the full embedding} degenerates to a local-degree quantity
for intrinsic dimension $d \ge 2$, and \textbf{conjecture}, with strong
empirical support, that the angular coordinate \rev{remains rank-faithful to
geodesic distance}---flat in both mode-count and graph size where the raw
embedding decays. The basis is \textbf{universal}: it preserves geodesics on any
substrate carrying genuine low-dimensional Riemannian geometry---random-geometric
lattices, manifold-embedding trajectories, and Wolfram-model hypergraph rewriting
alike---and fails correctly on Lorentzian causal sets and geometry-destroying
small-world graphs. \rev{Across $20$ independent emergent manifolds
($d \approx 1$--$3.6$), fidelity falls approximately linearly with intrinsic
dimension. Refined toward the continuum ($N$ up to $5\times10^{4}$), the basis
\emph{sharpens} on sampled Riemannian substrates (angular-fidelity Spearman
$+1.00$ with $\log N$ at $d{=}3$) yet \emph{decays} below a pre-registered floor
on a coordinate-free Wolfram-model manifold---a dissociation that recasts the
angular coordinate as a \emph{diagnostic} for whether a substrate's fine
structure stays manifold-like under refinement.}

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Spectral embeddings---Laplacian eigenmaps \cite{belkin2003}, diffusion and
commute-time maps \cite{coifman2006}---represent a graph by the low eigenvectors
of its Laplacian and underpin much of manifold learning and spectral clustering.
They carry a well-known pathology: on large geometric graphs the commute
(resistance) distance degenerates to a function of local degrees alone, losing
all global geometry \cite{vonluxburg2014}. Yet the most successful
spectral-clustering algorithm \cite{ng2002} \emph{row-normalizes} the
embedding---projects each node to the unit sphere---and works well\rev{. The
existing theory of that normalization concerns cluster geometry: it explains
why row-normalized embeddings of well-separated clusters concentrate in nearly
orthogonal directions on the sphere \cite{schiebinger2015,vonluxburg2008}. It
does not address metric geometry. \textbf{This paper asks what row-normalization
recovers of the graph's geodesic structure, and why.}}
% CUT: "with the normalization justified largely by heuristic" -- overstated
% given Schiebinger--Wainwright--Yu (2015); the framing is now "cluster theory
% exists, geodesic theory is ours."

Our answer is a clean decomposition. Write each node's low-mode embedding in polar
form, magnitude $\times$ direction. The \emph{magnitude} is exactly the degenerate
radial coordinate that von~Luxburg's theorem kills; the \emph{direction} carries
the graph's geodesic geometry. Row-normalization keeps the direction and discards
the magnitude---\rev{so beyond its cluster-separation role it is also the
geodesically correct projection}, and its fidelity is flat in both mode-count and
graph size precisely where the raw embedding decays.

This ``keep the scale-invariant direction'' operation recurs beyond
clustering\rev{---for example in polar-coordinate KV-cache quantization, which
transfers the scale-invariant direction and quantizes the radius separately
\cite{polarquant2025}.}
% CUT: the "preference geometry / aversion angle" clause -- uncited and
% unexplained; restore only with a citation and a sentence of substance.
It also admits a physical reading: in observer theory a bounded
observer perceives geometry only after coarse-graining a discrete substrate
\cite{wolfram2026}, and the angular projection is a candidate for that
coarse-graining. We keep that interpretation to the discussion
(\S\ref{sec:disc}) and let the spectral result stand on its own; the empirical
core is that the basis is \emph{universal}, preserving geodesics on any graph
carrying genuine low-dimensional Riemannian geometry, independent of how it was
generated.

\textbf{Contributions:} (1) the \textbf{Keep-the-Angle Principle}
(\S\ref{sec:thm})---a Radial-Degeneracy \emph{theorem} \rev{(for the full
embedding, as a corollary of \cite{vonluxburg2014})} and an
empirically-supported Angular-Preservation \emph{conjecture}; (2) a measurement
with controls (\S\ref{sec:results}); (3) universality across five substrate types
(\S\ref{sec:univ}); (4) a fidelity-vs-dimension scaling law (\S\ref{sec:law}); and
(5) two honest negatives (\S\ref{sec:neg}). We do \emph{not} claim to derive
physics; the core result is a basis-level fact about spectral embeddings.

\section{Background}
\textbf{Spectral geometry.} Laplacian eigenmaps \cite{belkin2003} and diffusion
maps \cite{coifman2006} embed a graph via low eigenvectors; the commute-time
embedding scales mode $k$ by $1/\sqrt{\lambda_k}$. \textbf{Commute-time
degeneracy.} von~Luxburg et al.\ \cite{vonluxburg2014} proved the commute distance
of a large geometric graph degenerates, \rev{$R(i,j)\to 1/k_i + 1/k_j$ ($k_i$ the
degree of node $i$)}; we read this backwards as the collapse of the \emph{radial}
coordinate to local degree. \textbf{Row-normalization.} Ng--Jordan--Weiss
\cite{ng2002} row-normalize the eigen-embedding---the angular projection we
study\rev{. Schiebinger, Wainwright and Yu \cite{schiebinger2015} give the
sharpest existing account: under a mixture model, row-normalized embeddings
concentrate clusters in orthogonal directions, explaining $k$-means'
post-normalization success; consistency of spectral clustering is treated in
\cite{vonluxburg2008}. Both analyses are about cluster separation on the sphere.
Our question---whether the normalized embedding preserves \emph{geodesic
distances} on a manifold-like graph---is complementary, and to our knowledge
open.} \textbf{Emergent geometry and observer theory.} In the
Wolfram model \cite{wolfram2002,wolfram2020,gorard2020} space is a hypergraph and
law is what a bounded observer perceives after coarse-graining the ruliad
\cite{wolfram2026}; that coarse-graining is left qualitative, and our angular
projection is a candidate for it (\S\ref{sec:disc}). \textbf{Hyperbolic
embedding.} Trees embed in $\mathbb{H}^2$ at low distortion
\cite{gromov1987,sarkar2011,nickel2017}; we test and reject a hyperbolic reframe
in \S\ref{sec:neg}.

\section{The Keep-the-Angle Principle}\label{sec:thm}
Let $G$ be connected on $n$ nodes with symmetric-normalized Laplacian
$L = I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}$, eigenpairs $(\lambda_k, v_k)$,
$0=\lambda_0<\lambda_1\le\cdots$. The \rev{low-mode spectral} embedding using the
lowest $m$ nontrivial modes places node $i$ at
$\Psi_i = \big(v_1(i)/\sqrt{\lambda_1},\dots,v_m(i)/\sqrt{\lambda_m}\big)$.
Write $\Psi_i = r_i\,\hat u_i$ with radius $r_i=\lVert\Psi_i\rVert$ and angle
$\hat u_i = \Psi_i/\lVert\Psi_i\rVert$.
\footnote{\color{teal}We call $\Psi$ ``commute-style'' rather than the commute-time
embedding proper: the classical commute embedding uses the pseudoinverse of the
\emph{unnormalized} Laplacian and all $n-1$ modes, so that
$\lVert\Psi_i-\Psi_j\rVert^2$ equals the resistance $R(i,j)$ exactly. Our
$\Psi$ is the truncated, normalized analogue standard in spectral clustering;
the distinction matters for Corollary~\ref{thm:radial} and is handled there.}

\paragraph{Intrinsic dimension \rev{and degree notation}.} Throughout, $d$ is the
intrinsic dimension of the substrate\rev{, and $k_i$ denotes the degree of node
$i$ (we reserve $d$ for dimension to avoid the collision $d$ vs.\ $d_i$)}: the
manifold dimension \emph{by construction} for the random-geometric benchmarks
(tori, king-lattices, sphere), and for emergent graphs the value at which the
three independent estimators of \rev{\S\ref{sec:methods}} (ball-growth, spectral
dimension, effective rank) mutually agree---reported with their inter-estimator
spread, with only low-spread graphs treated as manifolds. The regime $d\ge2$
refers to this measured intrinsic dimension; \S\ref{sec:law} confirms the $d=1$
boundary behaves as predicted.

{\color{teal}% entire theorem + remark are new in this revision
\begin{corollary}[Radial Degeneracy]\label{thm:radial}
Let $G_n$ be random geometric graphs of intrinsic dimension $d\ge2$ satisfying
the conditions of \cite{vonluxburg2014}, and let $\Phi_i$ be the \emph{full}
commute-time embedding built from the unnormalized Laplacian, so that
$\lVert\Phi_i\rVert^2 = L^{+}_{ii}$. As $n\to\infty$ the resistance distance
degenerates, $R(i,j)\to 1/k_i + 1/k_j$; since the only per-node content of this
additively separable form is the self-term $L^{+}_{ii}$, the radius satisfies
$\lVert\Phi_i\rVert \to 1/\sqrt{k_i}$. The radius of the full commute embedding
is therefore asymptotically geometry-free---a local-density coordinate carrying
no geodesic information.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Truncated, normalized radius]
Our experiments use the truncated radius $r_i=\lVert\Psi_i\rVert$ ($m$ lowest
modes of $L_{\mathrm{sym}}$), not $\lVert\Phi_i\rVert$. Transferring
Corollary~\ref{thm:radial} to this setting requires (i) the normalized analogue of
the degeneracy in \cite{vonluxburg2014} and (ii) control of the truncation
error, and we do not prove either here; we state the truncated statement as an
empirical observation. Numerically it holds tightly:
$\rho(r_i,1/\sqrt{k_i})=0.92$--$0.99$ and radius-only geodesic preservation
$\rho\le0.08$ (\S\ref{sec:verify}).
\end{remark}
}%

\begin{conjecture}[Angular Preservation]\label{conj:angle}
Row-normalization $\hat u_i = \Psi_i/\lVert\Psi_i\rVert$ deletes the degenerate
density factor of Corollary~\ref{thm:radial}, and \rev{the pairwise distances
among the resulting directions remain \emph{rank-faithful} to geodesic
distance---Spearman correlation bounded away from zero (empirically
$\approx0.9$)---\emph{uniformly in} the mode-count $m$ and size $n$. A stronger,
metric form of the conjecture---that the angular map is bi-Lipschitz to geodesic
distance with constants uniform in $m$ and $n$---is consistent with our data but
untested; our evidence is rank-based and cannot by itself bound metric
distortion.}
\end{conjecture}
% CUT: the bare "remains bi-Lipschitz" claim -- the headline conjecture now
% matches the evidence type (rank correlation), with bi-Lipschitz demoted to a
% stated stronger form.

\noindent\emph{Status.} Empirically strong (\S\ref{sec:results}) but \textbf{not
proven}. It rests on eigenmap-embedding results plus a heuristic; the honest gap
is that Corollary~\ref{thm:radial} explains why the radius \emph{dies} for
$d\ge2$, not by itself why the angle \emph{lives}---indeed the angular fidelity
persists ($\rho\approx0.88$--$0.90$) even on quasi-1D graphs where
Corollary~\ref{thm:radial} does not apply, so the angular half is carried by the
eigenmap-embedding mechanism, not degeneracy alone. \rev{That mechanism is
analyzable rather than mysterious: if the low $L_{\mathrm{sym}}$ eigenvectors
converge to Laplace--Beltrami eigenfunctions \cite{belkin2007,garciatrillos2018}
and eigenfunction coordinates furnish locally bi-Lipschitz charts of the
underlying manifold \cite{jones2010}, the angular map's geodesic fidelity should
follow from the chart geometry---the natural route to a proof of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:angle}, and this paper's central open problem.} Together
these are the \textbf{Keep-the-Angle Principle}: keep $\hat u_i$, discard $r_i$.

\paragraph{Dimension gating.} Corollary~\ref{thm:radial} is a $d\ge2$ phenomenon.
In (quasi-)1D, resistance equals path length, so the radius \emph{stays}
geodesic-informative and Corollary~\ref{thm:radial} correctly does not apply---a
prediction, not a failure, confirmed in \S\ref{sec:verify}.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

heat-kernel return probability $P(t)=\langle e^{-Lt}\rangle\sim t^{-d_s/2}$;

$\Rightarrow$ clean manifold, and the agreed value is our measured intrinsic
dimension $d$. \textbf{Angular metric.} Spearman $\rho$ between embedded pairwise
distances and true graph geodesics for: \emph{angle} (unit-normalized low-mode
embedding), \emph{random} ($m$ random modes; control), \emph{magnitude} (radius

\subsection{Emergent 2D geometry}
Random arity-2 rules yield clean but only $\approx 1.5$D manifolds (82
manifold-grade rules in a 20k sweep; none at $d\ge 2.5$). Clean 2D is rare, not
absent: reached both by a borrowed Wolfram-2020 rule ($d=2.07\pm0.01$, spread
$0.05$, angle-$\rho=0.82$) and by from-scratch evolutionary search ($\approx 3$
generations). The angular basis holds on genuine 2D emergent space.

\subsection{\rev{Stability across substrate evolution}}\label{sec:stability}
\rev{Universality across substrate \emph{types} (\S\ref{sec:results}) is a
statement about fixed graphs. The angular basis also persists as a substrate
\emph{evolves}. For each of $151$ qualifying Wolfram-model rules ($95$ arity-2,
$56$ arity-3) we embed successive rewrite snapshots on a fixed anchor set and
compare, between consecutive snapshots, the Spearman persistence of the
\emph{angular} pairwise structure (\texttt{consec}) against the persistence of
the underlying \emph{geodesic} structure itself (\texttt{churn})---the
substrate's own rate of change is the baseline the basis must keep pace with.
On all $143$ rules resolvable at protocol scale ($8$ never yield $\ge 3$
distinct snapshots and are excluded from the denominator but reported), the
angular structure tracks the evolving geometry at least as stably as the graph's
own geodesics, $\overline{\texttt{consec}} \ge 0.9\,\overline{\texttt{churn}}$:
a $143/143 = 100\%$ pass against a pre-registered $80\%$ bar, with zero
substrate-unstable rules. Over the longest time-lever (first vs.\ last snapshot)
angular persistence declines only gently with emergent dimension,}
\[
\text{slope } -0.029\text{/dim},\quad \text{Spearman } -0.48,\quad d\in[0.98,2.23],
\]
\rev{and this replicates independently in both arities (arity-2 $-0.028$/dim,
$\rho=-0.45$, $n=95$; arity-3 $-0.029$/dim, $\rho=-0.54$, $n=48$)---the same
mechanism, unchanged by moving from binary to ternary rewriting.}\footnote{%
Pre-registered as Amendment~2: an earlier capture hook could append the same
state once per crossed edge-count threshold, inflating consecutive stability
toward a spurious $1.0$. The corrected rule takes at most one snapshot per
generation with dynamic $2\times$ spacing; the numbers here are from that
harness.}

\subsection{\rev{Continuum survival, and a dissociation}}\label{sec:continuum}
\rev{Every result so far is at finite $N$ ($\approx2$--$4$k nodes, capped by
dense \texttt{eigh}); the ``uniform in $N$'' clause of Corollary~\ref{thm:radial}
and Conjecture~\ref{conj:angle} is untested at scale (\S\ref{sec:verify}). We
test it directly by refining each substrate toward the continuum with a sparse
eigensolver, $N \in \{2,5,10,20,50\}\times 10^3$, four seeds, $m=10$ modes,
$n=300$ anchors---well past the $\sim5$k dense ceiling. The two substrate
families dissociate. On genuine Riemannian substrates the basis
\emph{sharpens}: on random geometric graphs the angular fidelity is
flat-to-rising in $N$, most cleanly in the higher-dimensional case,}
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{RGG }d{=}3:\quad
&\text{angle-}\rho:\ 0.856 \to 0.912 \ \ (N:\,2\mathrm{k}\!\to\!50\mathrm{k}),\\
&\mathrm{Spearman}(\text{angle-}\rho,\log N) = +1.00,
\end{aligned}
\]
\rev{with $d{=}2$ likewise monotone ($+0.90$, $0.916\!\to\!0.929$): refining the
mesh makes the low modes resolve the geometry \emph{better}, not worse, and both
controls stay pinned ($\text{higher-mode }\rho\le0.05$, magnitude $\rho\le0.03$,
eigensolver residual $\sim10^{-14}$) at every $N$. The finite-$N$ evidence of
\S\S\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:verify} was not a small-graph artifact.}

\rev{The dissociation is with \emph{emergent} substrates. The
R\textsubscript{3D} Wolfram-model manifold---geometry with no latent
coordinates---\emph{decays} monotonically with refinement,
$\text{angle-}\rho: 0.749 \to 0.654$
($\mathrm{Spearman}=-1.00$), falling below a pre-registered floor of $0.75$; and
at the largest $N$ its magnitude control begins to break the clean angle/radius
separation that holds exactly on the RGGs (one $50$k seed crosses
$\rho_{\text{mag}}=0.33$ and is gated out). A sampled manifold refines to a
clean continuum; an emergent hypergraph, under this basis, does not---its fine
structure drifts away from a manifold as it grows. The coordinate-free
substrate---the one the observer-theory reading most needs---is exactly the one
where the basis fails in the limit. We read this not as a limitation but as a
result: the angular basis is a \emph{diagnostic} for whether a substrate's fine
structure stays manifold-like under refinement, and it returns different
verdicts for sampled ($2/3$ pass) and emergent ($1/1$ fail) geometry.}%
\footnote{Pre-registered (PREREG\_RUNG4) before the run; survival bar
$\text{angle-}\rho(N_{\max}) \ge \max(0.75,\,0.9\,\text{angle-}\rho(N_{\min}))$
on $\ge2/3$ substrates. Amendment~1, registered before any run: the

\section{Honest Negatives}\label{sec:neg}
\textbf{Not secretly hyperbolic (rejected).} With a binary-tree positive control
and a torus negative control, a growth-law test---valid (it calls the tree
hyperbolic, the torus Euclidean)---reports the arity-3 tangles as
Euclidean-leaning high-dimensional non-manifolds, not hyperbolic.
\textbf{Curvature is not dynamical (null).} Ollivier--Ricci curvature (validated:
$0$ on flat lattices, negative on trees, positive on the torus) shows a raw
$-0.98$ against update density that deflates to a tautology (update-count equals
degree, $\rho=+1.00$; hubs are negatively curved); controlling for degree, the
independent signal is partial-$\rho=-0.14$. This \emph{bounds the observer-theory
interpretation}---no emergent Einstein tensor---and leaves the spectral result
untouched.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:disc}
\textbf{The primary result is basis-level and substrate-agnostic.} The angular
coordinate of the low-Laplacian embedding preserves geodesics while the radial
coordinate is von-Luxburg-degenerate noise. This gives Ng--Jordan--Weiss
row-normalization a \rev{geodesic} justification \rev{complementary to the
cluster-separation theory of \cite{schiebinger2015}}, unifies it with
the scale-invariant direction of KV-cache compression \rev{\cite{polarquant2025}},
and holds \emph{universally} across substrates with genuine low-$d$ Riemannian
geometry, independent of graph origin. That the same basis works identically on
hypergraph-rewriting and \rev{sequential-trajectory} substrates is
\rev{evidence that scale-invariant angular coarse-graining is a general
mechanism by which} relational data carries geometric structure.
% CUT: "a general law of how relational data carries geometric structure" --
% softened; two substrate families are evidence for a mechanism, not a law.

\textbf{One interpretation is observer-theoretic.} In Wolfram's observer-relative
framework, the coarse-graining a bounded observer performs to perceive geometry is
exactly this angular projection---making ``the observer's basis'' a concrete
candidate for the coarse-graining the program leaves unspecified. We offer this as
motivation and bound it honestly: its strongest claim---dynamical, energy-
responsive curvature---is a null (\S\ref{sec:neg}). The core contribution stands
independent of the physics reading. Open problems: proving
Conjecture~\ref{conj:angle} \rev{(in its rank form, and ideally the bi-Lipschitz
strengthening)}; \rev{extending Corollary~\ref{thm:radial} to the truncated,
normalized radius actually used in practice;} synthesizing clean manifolds above
$d\approx2$; and a dimension-adaptive angular basis that tunes mode-count and
diffusion time to counter the fidelity decay. Keep the angle, drop the magnitude:
the basis-level fact is the contribution; the observer interpretation is where we
leave the door open.
