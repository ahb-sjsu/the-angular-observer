\begin{abstract}
The commute-time (resistance) embedding built from the low eigenvectors of a
graph Laplacian is a workhorse of spectral geometry, yet its distances are known
to \emph{degenerate} on large graphs \cite{vonluxburg2014}. We show the geometry
does not vanish---it migrates entirely into the \emph{angular} coordinate.
Writing each node's low-mode embedding in polar form (magnitude $\times$
direction), the direction preserves graph geodesics at Spearman
$\rho \approx 0.89$ on a reference manifold, while the magnitude preserves
$\rho \approx 0.03$ and a random-mode basis of equal size preserves
$\rho \approx 0.04$. Keeping only the angle is exactly the row-normalization of
Ng--Jordan--Weiss spectral clustering \cite{ng2002}, whose existing theory
concerns cluster separation \cite{schiebinger2015}; we give it a geodesic
justification. We show, as a corollary of von~Luxburg--Radl--Hein, that the
radial coordinate of the full embedding degenerates to a local-degree quantity
for intrinsic dimension $d \ge 2$, and \textbf{conjecture}, with strong empirical
support, that the angular coordinate remains rank-faithful to geodesic
distance---flat in both mode-count and graph size where the raw embedding decays.
The basis is \textbf{universal}: it preserves geodesics on any substrate carrying
genuine low-dimensional Riemannian geometry---random-geometric lattices,
manifold-embedding trajectories, and Wolfram-model hypergraph rewriting alike---
and fails correctly on Lorentzian causal sets and geometry-destroying small-world
graphs. Across $20$ independent emergent manifolds ($d \approx 1$--$3.6$),
fidelity falls approximately linearly with intrinsic dimension.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Spectral embeddings---Laplacian eigenmaps \cite{belkin2003}, diffusion and
commute-time maps \cite{coifman2006}---represent a graph by the low eigenvectors
of its Laplacian and underpin much of manifold learning and spectral clustering.
They carry a well-known pathology: on large geometric graphs the commute
(resistance) distance degenerates to a function of local degrees alone, losing
all global geometry \cite{vonluxburg2014}. Yet the most successful
spectral-clustering algorithm \cite{ng2002} \emph{row-normalizes} the
embedding---projects each node to the unit sphere---and works well. The existing
theory of that normalization concerns cluster geometry: it explains why
row-normalized embeddings of well-separated clusters concentrate in nearly
orthogonal directions on the sphere \cite{schiebinger2015,vonluxburg2008}. It
does not address metric geometry. \textbf{This paper asks what row-normalization
recovers of the graph's geodesic structure, and why.}

Our answer is a clean decomposition. Write each node's low-mode embedding in polar
form, magnitude $\times$ direction. The \emph{magnitude} is exactly the degenerate
radial coordinate that von~Luxburg's theorem kills; the \emph{direction} carries
the graph's geodesic geometry. Row-normalization keeps the direction and discards
the magnitude---so beyond its cluster-separation role it is also the geodesically
correct projection, and its fidelity is flat in both mode-count and graph size
precisely where the raw embedding decays.

This ``keep the scale-invariant direction'' operation has analogues beyond
clustering---for example polar-coordinate KV-cache quantization, which represents
key vectors in magnitude--direction form and quantizes the two parts separately
\cite{polarquant2025}. It also admits a physical reading: in observer theory a
bounded observer perceives geometry only after coarse-graining a discrete
substrate \cite{wolfram2026}, and the angular projection is a candidate for that
coarse-graining. We keep that interpretation to the discussion
(\S\ref{sec:disc}) and let the spectral result stand on its own; the empirical
core is that the basis is \emph{universal}, preserving geodesics on any graph
carrying genuine low-dimensional Riemannian geometry, independent of how it was
generated.

\textbf{Contributions:} (1) the \textbf{Keep-the-Angle Principle}
(\S\ref{sec:thm})---a Radial-Degeneracy \emph{theorem} (for the full embedding, as
a corollary of \cite{vonluxburg2014}) and an empirically-supported
Angular-Preservation \emph{conjecture}; (2) a measurement with controls
(\S\ref{sec:results}); (3) universality across five substrate types
(\S\ref{sec:univ}); (4) a fidelity-vs-dimension scaling law (\S\ref{sec:law}); and
(5) two honest negatives (\S\ref{sec:neg}). We do \emph{not} claim to derive
physics; the core result is a basis-level fact about spectral embeddings.

\section{Background}
\textbf{Spectral geometry.} Laplacian eigenmaps \cite{belkin2003} and diffusion
maps \cite{coifman2006} embed a graph via low eigenvectors; the commute-time
embedding scales mode $k$ by $1/\sqrt{\lambda_k}$. \textbf{Commute-time
degeneracy.} von~Luxburg et al.\ \cite{vonluxburg2014} proved the commute distance
of a large geometric graph degenerates, $R(i,j)\to 1/k_i + 1/k_j$ ($k_i$ the
degree of node $i$); we read this backwards as the collapse of the \emph{radial}
coordinate to local degree. \textbf{Row-normalization.} Ng--Jordan--Weiss
\cite{ng2002} row-normalize the eigen-embedding---the angular projection we study.
Schiebinger, Wainwright and Yu \cite{schiebinger2015} give the sharpest existing
account: under a mixture model, row-normalized embeddings concentrate clusters in
orthogonal directions, explaining $k$-means' post-normalization success;
consistency of spectral clustering is treated in \cite{vonluxburg2008}. Both
analyses are about cluster separation on the sphere. Our question---whether the
normalized embedding preserves \emph{geodesic distances} on a manifold-like
graph---is complementary, and to our knowledge open. \textbf{Emergent geometry and
observer theory.} In the Wolfram model \cite{wolfram2002,wolfram2020,gorard2020}
space is a hypergraph and law is what a bounded observer perceives after
coarse-graining the ruliad \cite{wolfram2026}; that coarse-graining is left
qualitative, and our angular projection is a candidate for it (\S\ref{sec:disc}).
\textbf{Hyperbolic embedding.} Trees embed in $\mathbb{H}^2$ at low distortion
\cite{gromov1987,sarkar2011,nickel2017}; we test and reject a hyperbolic reframe
in \S\ref{sec:neg}.

\section{The Keep-the-Angle Principle}\label{sec:thm}
Let $G$ be connected on $n$ nodes with symmetric-normalized Laplacian
$L = I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}$, eigenpairs $(\lambda_k, v_k)$,
$0=\lambda_0<\lambda_1\le\cdots$. The low-mode spectral embedding using the lowest
$m$ nontrivial modes places node $i$ at
$\Psi_i = \big(v_1(i)/\sqrt{\lambda_1},\dots,v_m(i)/\sqrt{\lambda_m}\big)$. Write
$\Psi_i = r_i\,\hat u_i$ with radius $r_i=\lVert\Psi_i\rVert$ and angle
$\hat u_i = \Psi_i/\lVert\Psi_i\rVert$.\footnote{We call $\Psi$
``commute-style'' rather than the commute-time embedding proper: the classical
commute embedding uses the pseudoinverse of the \emph{unnormalized} Laplacian and
all $n-1$ modes, so that $\lVert\Psi_i-\Psi_j\rVert^2$ equals the resistance
$R(i,j)$ exactly. Our $\Psi$ is the truncated, normalized analogue standard in
spectral clustering; the distinction matters for Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} and is
handled there.}

\paragraph{Intrinsic dimension and degree notation.} Throughout, $d$ is the
intrinsic dimension of the substrate, and $k_i$ denotes the degree of node $i$ (we
reserve $d$ for dimension to avoid the collision $d$ vs.\ $d_i$): the manifold
dimension \emph{by construction} for the random-geometric benchmarks (tori,
king-lattices, sphere), and for emergent graphs the value at which the three
independent estimators of \S\ref{sec:methods} (ball-growth, spectral dimension,
effective rank) mutually agree---reported with their inter-estimator spread, with
only low-spread graphs treated as manifolds. The regime $d\ge2$ refers to this
measured intrinsic dimension; \S\ref{sec:law} confirms the $d=1$ boundary behaves
as predicted.

\begin{theorem}[Radial Degeneracy]\label{thm:radial}
Let $G_n$ be random geometric graphs of intrinsic dimension $d\ge2$ satisfying the
conditions of \cite{vonluxburg2014}, and let $\Phi_i$ be the \emph{full}
commute-time embedding built from the unnormalized Laplacian, so that
$\lVert\Phi_i\rVert^2 = L^{+}_{ii}$. As $n\to\infty$ the resistance distance
degenerates, $R(i,j)\to 1/k_i + 1/k_j$; since the only per-node content of this
additively separable form is the self-term $L^{+}_{ii}$, the radius satisfies
$\lVert\Phi_i\rVert \to 1/\sqrt{k_i}$. The radius of the full commute embedding is
therefore asymptotically geometry-free---a local-density coordinate carrying no
geodesic information.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Truncated, normalized radius]
Our experiments use the truncated radius $r_i=\lVert\Psi_i\rVert$ ($m$ lowest
modes of $L_{\mathrm{sym}}$), not $\lVert\Phi_i\rVert$. Transferring
Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} to this setting requires (i) the normalized analogue of
the degeneracy in \cite{vonluxburg2014} and (ii) control of the truncation error,
and we do not prove either here; we state the truncated statement as an empirical
observation. Numerically it holds tightly: $\rho(r_i,1/\sqrt{k_i})=0.92$--$0.99$
and radius-only geodesic preservation $\rho\le0.08$ (\S\ref{sec:verify}).
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[Angular Preservation]\label{conj:angle}
Row-normalization $\hat u_i = \Psi_i/\lVert\Psi_i\rVert$ deletes the degenerate
density factor of Theorem~\ref{thm:radial}, and the pairwise distances among the
resulting directions remain \emph{rank-faithful} to geodesic distance---Spearman
correlation bounded away from zero (empirically $\approx0.9$)---\emph{uniformly
in} the mode-count $m$ and size $n$. A stronger, metric form of the
conjecture---that the angular map is bi-Lipschitz to geodesic distance with
constants uniform in $m$ and $n$---is consistent with our data but untested; our
evidence is rank-based and cannot by itself bound metric distortion.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent\emph{Status.} Empirically strong (\S\ref{sec:results}) but \textbf{not
proven}. It rests on eigenmap-embedding results plus a heuristic; the honest gap
is that Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} explains why the radius \emph{dies} for
$d\ge2$, not by itself why the angle \emph{lives}---indeed the angular fidelity
persists ($\rho\approx0.88$--$0.90$) even on quasi-1D graphs where
Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} does not apply, so the angular half is carried by the
eigenmap-embedding mechanism, not degeneracy alone. Together these are the
\textbf{Keep-the-Angle Principle}: keep $\hat u_i$, discard $r_i$.

\paragraph{Dimension gating.} Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} is a $d\ge2$ phenomenon.
In (quasi-)1D, resistance equals path length, so the radius \emph{stays}
geodesic-informative and Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} correctly does not apply---a
prediction, not a failure, confirmed in \S\ref{sec:verify}.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}
\textbf{Estimators.} Ball-growth $N(r)\sim r^d$; spectral dimension from the
heat-kernel return probability $P(t)=\langle e^{-Lt}\rangle\sim t^{-d_s/2}$;
effective rank via local-PCA participation ratio. Low inter-estimator spread
$\Rightarrow$ clean manifold, and the agreed value is our measured intrinsic
dimension $d$. \textbf{Angular metric.} Spearman $\rho$ between embedded pairwise
distances and true graph geodesics for: \emph{angle} (unit-normalized low-mode
embedding), \emph{random} ($m$ random modes; control), \emph{magnitude} (radius
only), \emph{euclid} (classical 2D MDS). \textbf{Uncertainty.} Each $\rho$ is a
Spearman correlation over the $\binom{a}{2}$ pairs of $a$ anchor nodes ($a=150$
unless noted; $11{,}175$ pairs), with bootstrap $95\%$ CIs over pair resampling
($2000$ resamples). On the 2-torus ($n=2000$) the angular basis gives
$\rho=0.894$, CI $[0.888,0.899]$, and $0.897\pm0.012$ across $8$ independent graph
draws; the magnitude control gives $0.006$ $[0.000,0.025]$ and the random-mode
control $0.037$ $[0.018,0.055]$. \textbf{Substrates.} Arity-2/3 hypergraph
rewriting (clique-expanded), plus tori, king-lattices, Poisson causal sets, and a
swiss-roll embedding trajectory: a synthetic 2D manifold (spiral angle
$u\in[1.5\pi,4.5\pi]$, height $v\in[0,20]$) carried into $\mathbb{R}^{12}$ by a
random orthogonal map with isotropic Gaussian noise ($\sigma=0.03$), connected as
a $k=10$ nearest-neighbour graph over $n=2000$ points---the analogue of embedding
a corpus line-by-line onto a low-dimensional semantic manifold and joining nearest
lines.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{The core result}
Estimators recover ground-truth dimension within $\approx 0.3$. On a 2-torus the
polar decomposition of the low-mode embedding (Fig.~\ref{fig:core}) gives:

\begin{tabular}{rccc}

$m$ & full (mag.$\times$dir.) & \textbf{angle only} & magnitude only \\
\midrule

40 & 0.517 & \textbf{0.928} & 0.034 \\

The angle carries the geometry ($\approx 0.92$, flat in $m$); the magnitude is

\subsection{Emergent 2D geometry}
Random arity-2 rules yield clean but only $\approx 1.5$D manifolds (82
manifold-grade rules in a 20k sweep; none at $d\ge 2.5$). Clean 2D is rare, not
absent: reached both by a borrowed Wolfram-2020 rule ($d=2.07\pm0.01$, spread
$0.05$, angle-$\rho=0.82$) and by from-scratch evolutionary search ($\approx 3$
generations). The angular basis holds on genuine 2D emergent space.

\subsection{Universality across substrates}\label{sec:univ}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
substrate & meas.\ $d$ & angle-$\rho$ & random & euclid \\
\midrule
2-torus (reference) & 2 & 0.89 & 0.01 & 0.67 \\
king-lattice & 2 & 0.82 & 0.01 & 0.95 \\
swiss-roll trajectory & 2 & 0.93 & 0.01 & 0.99 \\
emergent $R_{3D}$ (Wolfram) & 2.07 & 0.82 & 0.01 & 0.81 \\
causal set (Lorentzian) & 3.9 & 0.57 & 0.01 & 0.55 \\
small-world (destroyed) & 3.0$^{*}$ & 0.45 & 0.00 & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Substrates with genuine low-$d$ Riemannian geometry all show the effect,
independent of graph origin; the metric fails correctly where geometry is
Lorentzian or destroyed. $^{*}$For the small-world graph the three dimension
estimators do \emph{not} strongly disagree (spread $0.21$, comparable to the clean
lattice's $0.22$); rather, $3\%$ rewiring inflates the measured dimension from the
construction value $2$ to $\approx3$ and collapses angle-$\rho$ to $0.45$. Here the
fidelity metric itself---not the spread criterion---detects the destroyed
geometry: this is the predicted failure, not an exception.

Two caveats keep the universality claim honest. First, on the two flat substrates
(king-lattice, swiss-roll) classical MDS \emph{beats} the angular basis ($0.95$
and $0.99$ vs.\ $0.82$ and $0.93$): MDS wins whenever the manifold embeds
near-isometrically in low-dimensional Euclidean space, while the angle wins where
topology obstructs such an embedding (the torus: $0.89$ vs.\ $0.67$) and, unlike
MDS, is uniform in $m$ and $n$ (\S\ref{sec:verify}). Universality here means the
angular basis never \emph{loses} the geometry on a genuine low-$d$ Riemannian
substrate---not that it dominates every baseline on every substrate. Second, the
swiss-roll trajectory is the sequential-embedding case: the angular basis is the
same whether the manifold comes from hypergraph rewriting or sequential-trajectory
data.

\subsection{The scaling law}\label{sec:law}
Across 20 independent emergent manifolds ($d\approx 1$--$3.6$),
\[
\text{angle-}\rho \;\approx\; 1.09 - 0.157\,d, \qquad
\text{slope } -0.157 \pm 0.028,\quad R^2 = 0.64,\quad d\in[1.0,3.6].
\]
(Spearman $-0.881$ between $\rho$ and $d$ serves as a distribution-free
monotonicity check; the linear fit itself should be judged by its slope
uncertainty and $R^2$.) The fit is \emph{empirical and local}: $\rho$ is a rank
correlation bounded by 1, so the linear form holds only within the measured
range---the intercept $1.09$ is an extrapolation, not a literal $d\to0$ fidelity,
and the slope may vary with substrate roughness. Within range, fidelity is highest
at low dimension and softens toward $\approx0.56$ at $d\approx3.4$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:law}); the dimension-gated degeneracy of \S\ref{sec:thm} is the
mechanism.

\subsection{Verifying Theorem 1 and Conjecture 1}\label{sec:verify}
angle-$\rho$ is flat in graph size while the full commute distance decays, and
the radius tracks degree exactly ($\rho(\lVert\Psi_i\rVert,1/\sqrt{k_i})=
0.92$--$0.99$; radius-only $\rho\le0.08$). Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} holds cleanly
at $d=3$ ($\rho(R,1/k_i+1/k_j)\to0.98$); $d=2$ is borderline (log corrections);
the quasi-1D case correctly shows no degeneracy, as the dimension gating predicts.
Conjecture~\ref{conj:angle}: in an $m$-sweep at $n=4000$, angle-$\rho$ is flat
$\approx0.93$ across $m=5$--$80$ while the full embedding decays $0.773\to0.437$.

\section{Honest Negatives}\label{sec:neg}
\textbf{Not secretly hyperbolic (rejected).} With a binary-tree positive control
and a torus negative control, a growth-law test---valid (it calls the tree
hyperbolic, the torus Euclidean)---reports the arity-3 tangles as
Euclidean-leaning high-dimensional non-manifolds, not hyperbolic.
\textbf{Curvature is not dynamical (null).} Ollivier--Ricci curvature (validated:
$0$ on flat lattices, negative on trees, positive on the torus) shows a raw
$-0.98$ against update density that deflates to a tautology (update-count equals
degree, $\rho=+1.00$; hubs are negatively curved); controlling for degree, the
independent signal is partial-$\rho=-0.14$. This \emph{bounds the observer-theory
interpretation}---no emergent Einstein tensor---and leaves the spectral result
untouched.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:disc}
\textbf{The primary result is basis-level and substrate-agnostic.} The angular
coordinate of the low-Laplacian embedding preserves geodesics while the radial
coordinate is von-Luxburg-degenerate noise. This gives Ng--Jordan--Weiss
row-normalization a geodesic justification complementary to the cluster-separation
theory of \cite{schiebinger2015}, unifies it with the scale-invariant direction of
KV-cache compression \cite{polarquant2025}, and holds \emph{universally} across
substrates with genuine low-$d$ Riemannian geometry, independent of graph origin.
That the same basis works identically on hypergraph-rewriting and
sequential-trajectory substrates is evidence that scale-invariant angular
coarse-graining is a general mechanism by which relational data carries geometric
structure.

\textbf{One interpretation is observer-theoretic.} In Wolfram's observer-relative
framework, the coarse-graining a bounded observer performs to perceive geometry is
exactly this angular projection---making ``the observer's basis'' a concrete
candidate for the coarse-graining the program leaves unspecified. We offer this as
motivation and bound it honestly: its strongest claim---dynamical, energy-
responsive curvature---is a null (\S\ref{sec:neg}). The core contribution stands
independent of the physics reading. Open problems: proving
Conjecture~\ref{conj:angle} (in its rank form, and ideally the bi-Lipschitz
strengthening); extending Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} to the truncated, normalized
radius actually used in practice; synthesizing clean manifolds above $d\approx2$;
and a dimension-adaptive angular basis that tunes mode-count and diffusion time to
counter the fidelity decay. Keep the angle, drop the magnitude: the basis-level
fact is the contribution; the observer interpretation is where we leave the door
open.
