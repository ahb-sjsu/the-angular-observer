\begin{abstract}
A bounded observer embedded in a discrete substrate perceives not the substrate
but a coarse-graining of it. Wolfram's physics program makes this the
load-bearing move---coherent law is \emph{what survives} an observer's
coarse-graining of the hypergraph---but leaves the coarse-graining unspecified.
We give it an explicit, measurable form and show it is not special to physics.
Writing the graph normalized-Laplacian eigen-embedding in polar form (magnitude
$\times$ direction), the \emph{angular} coordinates of the low-eigenvalue
subspace carry the graph's geodesic geometry while the \emph{radial} coordinate
is asymptotically geometry-free. Keeping only the angle---the row-normalization
of Ng--Jordan--Weiss spectral clustering, and the scale-invariant direction of
PolarQuant compression---recovers geodesics at Spearman $\rho \approx 0.93$ on a
reference manifold, versus $\rho \approx 0$ for a random-mode basis of equal size
and $\rho \approx 0.03$ for the magnitude alone. We prove, via the
von~Luxburg--Radl--Hein resistance-degeneracy theorem, that the radial
coordinate must degenerate to a local-degree quantity for intrinsic dimension
$d \ge 2$, and verify that the angular coordinate's fidelity is instead flat in
both mode-count and graph size. The effect is \textbf{universal across substrates
carrying genuine low-dimensional Riemannian geometry}---Wolfram-model rewriting,
king-move lattices, and manifold-embedding trajectories alike---and fails
correctly on Lorentzian causal sets and geometry-destroying small-world graphs.
Across $20$ independent emergent manifolds ($d \approx 1$--$3.6$), fidelity obeys
$\rho \approx \text{const} - 0.157\,d$ (Spearman $-0.881$). Finally we report a
clean negative: emergent curvature is \emph{not} dynamical beyond graph
structure, so this is a result about the observer, not the Einstein tensor.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Every account of emergent space shares a shape: a structureless discrete
substrate, a bounded agent that cannot resolve it in full, and a smooth geometry
that appears at the agent's resolution. Wolfram's hypergraph-rewriting program
\cite{wolfram2002,wolfram2020,gorard2020} is the most developed instance---space
is a hypergraph, time is rewriting, and physical law is what a computationally
bounded observer perceives after coarse-graining the ruliad \cite{wolfram2026}.
The observer does the decisive work, yet the coarse-graining is described only
qualitatively.

We make it explicit. Our starting point is a decomposition that recurs,
apparently independently, in four settings: spectral clustering
\cite{ng2002} row-normalizes the Laplacian embedding (keeps the direction);
PolarQuant KV-cache compression transfers the scale-invariant direction; the
``aversion angle'' of preference geometry transfers across decision domains while
magnitude is domain-specific; and observer theory \cite{wolfram2026} holds that
perceived geometry is a coarse-graining artifact. We show these are the same
operation---\emph{keep the angle of the low-eigenvalue Laplacian embedding, drop
the radius}---and that it has a precise geometric meaning. Contributions: the
Keep-the-Angle theorem (\S\ref{sec:thm}); a measurement with controls
(\S\ref{sec:results}); universality (\S\ref{sec:univ}); a scaling law
(\S\ref{sec:law}); and two honest negatives (\S\ref{sec:neg}). We do \emph{not}
claim to derive general relativity, nor that compressibility is ontologically
fundamental; we claim that scale-invariant angular coarse-graining is a
universal, measurable model of the bounded observer.

\section{Background}
\textbf{Emergent geometry.} Dimension is estimated from ball growth
$V(r)\sim r^d$ and curvature from its correction \cite{gorard2020}; our
estimators are of this family. \textbf{Spectral geometry.} Laplacian eigenmaps
\cite{belkin2003} and diffusion maps \cite{coifman2006} embed via low
eigenvectors; Ng--Jordan--Weiss \cite{ng2002} row-normalize---the angular
projection we study. \textbf{Commute-time degeneracy.} von~Luxburg et al.\
\cite{vonluxburg2014} proved the commute distance of a large geometric graph
degenerates, $R(i,j)\to 1/d_i + 1/d_j$; we read this backwards as the collapse of
the \emph{radial} coordinate. \textbf{Hyperbolic embedding.} Trees embed in
$\mathbb{H}^2$ at low distortion \cite{gromov1987,sarkar2011,nickel2017}; we test
and reject a hyperbolic reframe in \S\ref{sec:neg}.

\section{The Keep-the-Angle Theorem}\label{sec:thm}
Let $G$ be connected on $n$ nodes with symmetric-normalized Laplacian
$L = I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}$, eigenpairs $(\lambda_k, v_k)$,
$0=\lambda_0<\lambda_1\le\cdots$. The commute-time embedding using the lowest $m$
nontrivial modes places node $i$ at
$\Psi_i = \big(v_1(i)/\sqrt{\lambda_1},\dots,v_m(i)/\sqrt{\lambda_m}\big)$.
Write $\Psi_i = r_i\,\hat u_i$ with radius $r_i=\lVert\Psi_i\rVert$ and angle
$\hat u_i = \Psi_i/\lVert\Psi_i\rVert$.

\begin{theorem}[Keep-the-Angle, informal]
For graphs of intrinsic dimension $d\ge 2$, as $n\to\infty$:
(1) \emph{(radius, proven)} the full commute distance degenerates to an
additively separable degree function \cite{vonluxburg2014}; its surviving
per-node coordinate is the radius, with $r_i\to 1/\sqrt{d_i}$ ($d_i$ the degree),
hence geometry-free. (2) \emph{(angle, conjectured)} row-normalization deletes
that degenerate density factor, leaving the direction of the low-mode eigenmap,
which embeds the manifold and---empirically---remains bi-Lipschitz to geodesic
distance uniformly in $m$ and $n$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\textbf{Proven vs.\ conjectured.} Part (1) is a corollary of
\cite{vonluxburg2014}, verified below ($\rho(r_i,\text{degree})=0.92$--$0.99$).
Part (2) rests on eigenmap-embedding results plus a heuristic and is an
\emph{empirically strong conjecture}. The honest gap: von~Luxburg explains why
the radius \emph{dies} for $d\ge 2$, not by itself why the angle \emph{lives}---
indeed the angular fidelity persists ($\rho\approx0.88$--$0.90$) even on quasi-1D
graphs where no radial degeneracy occurs, so the angular half is carried by the
eigenmap mechanism, not degeneracy alone. \textbf{Dimension gating:} in
(quasi-)1D, resistance equals path length, so the radius stays
geodesic-informative and part (1) correctly does not apply.

\section{Methods}
\textbf{Estimators.} Ball-growth $N(r)\sim r^d$; spectral dimension from the
heat-kernel return probability $P(t)=\langle e^{-Lt}\rangle\sim t^{-d_s/2}$;
effective rank via local-PCA participation ratio. Low inter-estimator spread
$\Rightarrow$ clean manifold. \textbf{Observer metric.} Spearman $\rho$ between
embedded pairwise distances and true graph geodesics for: \emph{angle} (unit-
normalized low-mode embedding), \emph{random} ($m$ random modes; control),
\emph{magnitude} (radius only), \emph{euclid} (classical 2D MDS).
\textbf{Substrates.} Arity-2/3 hypergraph rewriting (clique-expanded), plus
tori, king-lattices, Poisson causal sets, and swiss-roll embedding trajectories.

\subsection{Emergent 2D geometry}
Random arity-2 rules yield clean but only $\approx 1.5$D manifolds (82
manifold-grade rules in a 20k sweep; none at $d\ge 2.5$). Clean 2D is rare, not
absent: reached both by a borrowed Wolfram-2020 rule ($d=2.07\pm0.01$, spread
$0.05$, angle-$\rho=0.82$) and by from-scratch evolutionary search ($\approx 3$
generations). The principle holds on genuine 2D emergent space.

Substrates with genuine low-$d$ Riemannian geometry all show the effect,
independent of graph origin; the metric fails correctly where geometry is
Lorentzian or destroyed. The swiss-roll trajectory is the sequential-embedding
case: the observer basis is the same whether the manifold comes from physics or
cultural sequential data.

\subsection{The scaling law}\label{sec:law}
Across 20 independent emergent manifolds ($d\approx 1$--$3.6$),
\[
\text{angle-}\rho \;\approx\; \text{const} - 0.157\,d, \qquad
\text{Spearman} = -0.881 .
\]
Fidelity is highest at low dimension and softens as the space grows
higher-dimensional (Fig.~\ref{fig:law}); the dimension-gated degeneracy of
\S\ref{sec:thm} is the mechanism.

\subsection{Theorem verification}
angle-$\rho$ is flat in graph size while the full commute distance decays, and
the radius tracks degree exactly ($\rho(\lVert\Psi_i\rVert,1/\sqrt{d_i})=
0.92$--$0.99$; radius-only $\rho\le0.08$). The $d=3$ degeneracy is clean
($\rho(R,1/d_i+1/d_j)\to0.98$); $d=2$ is borderline (log corrections); the
quasi-1D case correctly shows no degeneracy.

\section{Honest Negatives}\label{sec:neg}
\textbf{Not secretly hyperbolic (rejected).} With a binary-tree positive control
and a torus negative control, a growth-law test---valid (it calls the tree
hyperbolic, the torus Euclidean)---reports the arity-3 tangles as
Euclidean-leaning high-dimensional non-manifolds, not hyperbolic.
\textbf{Curvature is not dynamical (null).} Ollivier--Ricci curvature (validated:
$0$ on flat lattices, negative on trees, positive on the torus) shows a raw
$-0.98$ against update density that deflates to a tautology (update-count equals
degree, $\rho=+1.00$; hubs are negatively curved); controlling for degree, the
independent signal is partial-$\rho=-0.14$. No evidence of an emergent Einstein
tensor.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The angular observer unifies four ``keep the direction, drop the magnitude''
moves and grounds them in one fact: for a bounded observer of a $d\ge2$
substrate, the radial (density) coordinate is noise and the angular (geometric)
coordinate is signal. In Wolfram's observer-relative ontology, identifying the
observer's coarse-graining basis is a substantive contribution to observer
theory, even though it does not reach the dynamical-curvature claim. The
identical behavior on physics-rewriting and cultural-sequential substrates
supports the reading that scale-invariant angular coarse-graining is a general
law of how bounded systems represent geometric structure. Open problems: proving
the angular half; synthesizing clean manifolds above $d\approx2$; and a
theorem-guided observer that tunes mode-count and diffusion time to the
substrate's dimension to counter the fidelity decay. Keep the angle, drop the
magnitude: it is the observer's basis, not the world's ontology.
