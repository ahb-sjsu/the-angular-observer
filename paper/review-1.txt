Constructive Critique & Areas for ImprovementA. The "Theorem" vs. "Conjecture" NomenclatureIn Section 3, you introduce "Theorem 1 (Keep-the-Angle, informal)".  However, within the same block, you explicitly state that part 1 (the radial coordinate) is proven via von Luxburg, but part 2 (the angular coordinate) is conjectured.  Recommendation: In formal mathematical literature, labeling a partially conjectured statement as a "Theorem" is highly unorthodox and will likely draw harsh criticism from reviewers. Consider renaming this to the "Keep-the-Angle Principle" or explicitly separating it into "Theorem 1 (Radial Degeneracy)" and "Conjecture 1 (Angular Preservation)".B. Audience and FramingThe paper weaves together standard spectral geometry (Laplacian eigenmaps, diffusion maps) with Wolfram's observer-relative framework.  While you clearly state that the observer physics is a motivating interpretation, traditional spectral graph theorists or machine learning researchers might find the hypergraph physics terminology distracting.  Recommendation: Ensure the core machine learning and spectral geometry audience (those who care about Ng-Jordan-Weiss clustering) doesn't lose the mathematical plot. You might want to compartmentalize the observer theory slightly more, perhaps confining it strictly to the discussion section rather than introducing it heavily in the abstract and intro.  C. Scaling Law ClarityIn Section 5.4, you present the scaling law for angular fidelity as $\rho \approx \text{const} - 0.157 d$.  Recommendation: The definition or value of "const" is left ambiguous in the text. Providing the exact intercept value or briefly explaining why the constant might vary depending on the substrate would make this result much stronger.  D. Mathematical FormalityThe draft is highly readable, but it occasionally trades mathematical rigor for conceptual narrative.For instance, the "dimension gating" concept mentioned in Section 3 is intuitive, but formalizing exactly how intrinsic dimension $d\ge2$ is defined and measured in your continuous limits would preempt reviewer questions.  Overall, the core basis-level fact—that you should keep the angle and drop the magnitude—stands strong independent of the physics reading you apply to it.
